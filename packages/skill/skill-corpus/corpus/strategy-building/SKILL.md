# Strategy Building — Roger Martin Framework

## Core Distinction

| Plan | Strategy |
|------|----------|
| List of activities you intend to do | Integrative set of choices positioning you to win |
| Comfortable — you control the inputs (costs) | Angst-making — depends on customers choosing you |
| Assumes the future = present | Acknowledges fundamental uncertainty |
| Guarantees nothing (you can "lose optimally") | Gives the best possible chance of winning |
| No coherence between activities | Internally coherent theory of how to win |

**Key insight:** "Most strategic planning in business has nothing to do with strategy. It's just a list of activities that sound good."

**Closing logic:** "If you plan, that's a way to guarantee losing. If you do strategy, it gives you the best possible chance of winning."

## The Strategy Cascade (5 Questions)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. What is our winning aspiration?  │  ← The goal / outcome
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Where will we play?              │  ← Playing field / segment
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. How will we win?                 │  ← Competitive advantage / theory
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. What capabilities must we have?  │  ← Resources needed
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5. What management systems needed?  │  ← Processes / enablers
└─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Write the whole strategy on **a single page**.

## Key Ending Logic: "What Must Be True"

After laying out the cascade, **the critical final step** is to lay out the logic explicitly:

1. **Articulate the assumptions** — "What would have to be true about ourselves, about the industry, about competition, about customers for this strategy to work?"
2. **Watch and tweak as you go** — Strategy is a journey. When reality contradicts your assumptions, adapt.
3. **Refine iteratively** — Use the "what must be true" logic as a mechanism for honing the strategy over time.

This is what makes strategy different from a plan — it acknowledges uncertainty and builds in a feedback loop.

## Example: Southwest Airlines

- **Aspiration:** Be a substitute for Greyhound — convenient point-to-point travel at a modest premium
- **Where to play:** Short-haul, point-to-point routes (not hub-and-spoke)
- **How to win:** Lowest cost structure → lowest prices
  - One aircraft type (737) — simpler gates, training, maintenance
  - No meals (short flights don't need them)
  - Direct booking (no travel agents)
  - No time on ground (only make money in the air)
- **Result:** Chewed up the major carriers who were "playing to play" (not playing to win)

## How to Escape the Planning Trap

1. **Accept the angst** — You can't prove a strategy will succeed in advance. That's not bad management; it's great leadership.
2. **Lay out the logic clearly** — Explicit "what must be true" statements enable you to watch and adapt.
3. **Keep it simple** — One page. Where to play, how to win, capabilities, systems, why it achieves the aspiration.
4. **Iterate** — Strategy is a journey. Tweak as you learn.
